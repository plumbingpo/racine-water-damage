Why Hire a Professional for Flood Cleanup?

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Debris in a gutter or storm drain blocks proper flow, so clean them out regularly
  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • Being outside a designated flood zone does not rule out flooding, so think about flood coverage
  • Wherever flooding is likely, get appliances and electrical equipment up above floor level
  • Install backwater valves on sewer lines to stop sewage from backing up into your home
  • A battery-powered backup sump pump keeps water moving if the electricity fails in a storm

Signs It Is Time for Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • Storm damage that lets rain in through the roof or walls
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Standing water anywhere on your property, whatever the source
  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Flooring that squelches when you walk on it and walls damp to the touch
  • Receding water that leaves muddy residue and debris behind
