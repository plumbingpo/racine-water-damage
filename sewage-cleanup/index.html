A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Racine, Racine Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
